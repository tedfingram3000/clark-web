<div style="font-size:20px;font-weight:700;color:#0B1C33;line-height:1.3;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Bullpen hope then bullpen reality</div>
<div style="font-size:13px;color:#707070;margin:0 0 14px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">It&#8217;s not good when the pitchers of The Summer Game get worse in the summer</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;"><b>Who lost the close games?</b> Measured against other teams, the Cardinals' bullpen came out the furthest behind, mostly after May. Over 54 games, though, chance could account for much of it.<sup>1</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The Cardinals went 24-30 in one-run games in 2026. They played 54 of them, tied with the Dodgers for the most in the majors.<sup>2</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">MLB tracks each team's chance to win after every play. When a play changes that chance, the swing goes to the hitters if they were batting and to the pitcher on the mound if he gave it up. Over a season, those swings add up to wins gained or lost.<sup>3</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">In 2026, one-run games were low-scoring. They averaged about 7.6 runs a game, against 9.5 in other games.<sup>4</sup> In those games, the pitchers won more of the battles. Every lineup shows a minus, and 24 of the 30 bullpens show a plus. That reflects the kind of game, not bullpens being better than hitters, so we compare bullpens with bullpens and lineups with lineups, and we put every team on 54 one-run games.<sup>5</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Over those 54 games, the Cardinals' bullpen cost them about 0.3 wins. A typical bullpen gained about 2.9. That left the Cardinals about 3.3 wins behind. Their starters and their lineup were close to typical.<sup>5</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Chance matters here. Over 54 games, luck alone can move a bullpen's number by about 2.6 wins either way. The bullpen gap is a direction, not proof.<sup>6</sup></p>

<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Before and after June</div>

<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Riley O'Brien, the closer, got the most big spots, as closers usually do. Going into June, he and George Soriano had ERAs near 3.20,<sup>10</sup> so the Cardinals gave them the big spots. Then their ERAs rose, while Ryne Stanek's and Justin Bruihl's fell. Gordon Graceffo had the best ERA through May and got the fewest big spots of the six.</p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Over about 40 to 50 innings, ERA can swing a lot by chance, and managers weigh more than ERA.</p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">What it adds up to</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The Cardinals' close-game problem was mostly a bullpen problem, and mostly a summer one. Through May, their bullpen ran about 1.1 wins ahead of a typical one in one-run games. From June on, it fell about 5.2 wins behind. After May, the biggest spots went to O'Brien and Soriano, and both pitched worse from then on. The lineup and the rotation held their own. One season is a small sample, so chance could explain much of the gap. Still, it points to the bullpen first.</p>

<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">These numbers divide the one-run record among the hitters, the starters and the bullpen. They do not explain why it happened, and we cannot say whether any game would have turned out differently with another pitcher on the mound. The win chances are MLB's estimates. A pitcher's number includes the defense behind him.</p>

<p style="font-size:11px;color:#707070;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;"><sup>1</sup> Each part of the team is compared with the same part of the other 29 teams, per one-run game, scaled to the Cardinals' 54 one-run games. <p style="font-size:11px;color:#707070;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;"><sup>2</sup> MLB's official data, the Stats API: https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/schedule?sportId=1&amp;season=2026&amp;gameType=R&amp;hydrate=linescore gives the final score of all 2,429 regular-season games. Each team's count and record are in https://data.700clark.com/season-2026/data/closeloss-teams-2026.csv.</p>
<p style="font-size:11px;color:#707070;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;"><sup>3</sup> For each game, https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/game/{gamePk}/winProbability lists every play with homeTeamWinProbability, the home team's chance to win after it, in percent. A play's swing is that number minus the same number after the play before; the first play's swing is that number minus 50. The batting team's swing goes to its lineup. The same swing, sign flipped, goes to the fielding team's pitcher on the play: the starter if he is the team's first pitcher of the game, the bullpen if not. Add each team's swings in a game and divide by 100 to get wins. In every game, the winner's three parts add up to 0.5 and the loser's to -0.5. MLB's feed also lists its own per-play value, homeTeamWinProbabilityAdded. We do not use it, because it leaves out changes between plays, such as a stolen base during a plate appearance. It differs from the change above by more than a tenth of a point on 1,808 of 58,787 plays, and in 652 of 776 games its values do not add up to the result. Every team's three numbers in every one-run game, with MLB's game id (gamePk), are in https://data.700clark.com/season-2026/data/closeloss-games-2026.csv.</p>
<p style="font-size:11px;color:#707070;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;"><sup>4</sup> From the same final scores: both teams' runs per game, averaged over the 668 one-run games and the 1,761 others.</p>
<p style="font-size:11px;color:#707070;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;"><sup>5</sup> A team's number per game is its total over its one-run games divided by how many it played. "Typical" is the middle of the other 29 teams' per-game numbers, times 54. For bullpens that is 0.0544 per game, or about 2.9 wins. The Cardinals' bullpen total was -0.34. An opener counts as the starter, and the pitchers behind him, or behind a starter who left early, count as bullpen. Every play's swing for the hitters is the same swing against the pitchers, so across both teams the parts add up to zero. Over all 1,336 team-games in one-run games, the average per game was -0.100 for lineups, +0.046 for starters and +0.054 for bullpens. All 30 lineups' numbers are below zero, and 24 of the 30 bullpens' are above.</p>
