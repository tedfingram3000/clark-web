<div style="font-size:20px;font-weight:700;color:#0B1C33;line-height:1.3;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Alec Burleson for the win</div>
<div style="font-size:13px;color:#707070;margin:0 0 14px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">With a hat tip to the RISP who made it possible</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;"><b>How did Alec Burleson lead the majors in RBI?</b> He came to the plate with men on base more than any other qualified hitter, and in 2026 he hit much better with them on.</p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Burleson drove in 116 runs in 2026. Baltimore's Pete Alonso was next in the majors, with 113. Sal Stewart of the Reds was second in the National League, with 112. Teammate Jordan Walker was seventh in the majors, with 102.<sup>1</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Burleson is the first Cardinal to lead the majors in RBI since Joe Torre drove in 137 in 1971. He is the first to lead the National League since Albert Pujols drove in 118 in 2010.<sup>2</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Burleson bats left-handed. The last left-handed Cardinal to lead the majors was Enos Slaughter, with 130 in 1946. The last to lead the National League was Stan Musial, with 109 in 1956. Burleson's 116 are the most by a left-handed Cardinal since Musial drove in 126 in 1954.<sup>2</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">His bat alone does not explain the title. His .816 OPS tied for 34th among the 135 qualified hitters in the majors, and tied for 21st among the 68 in the National League.<sup>3</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">More chances than anyone</div>

<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Burleson batted with men on base in 54% of his plate appearances, the highest share of any qualified hitter. The typical qualified hitter batted with men on 44% of the time.<sup>4</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">That share is high even for an RBI leader. Since 2000, the typical league or major-league RBI leader batted with men on 49% of the time. The last one with a higher share than Burleson's was Alex Rodriguez in 2007, at 56%.<sup>5</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Burleson batted third in 119 of his 150 starts. Ahead of him, JJ Wetherholt led off in 135 starts and Iv&#225;n Herrera batted second in 147. Both got on base more often than the typical qualified hitter: .339 and .353, against .331.<sup>6</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Better with men on</div>
<div style="background-color:#F4F4F4;border:1px solid #E0E0E0;border-left:4px solid #0B1C33;border-radius:4px;padding:12px 16px;margin:4px 0 14px;">

<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">A typical qualified hitter's OPS rose .040 with men on. Burleson's rose .159, the 22nd-biggest rise among the 135. His rise was smaller in 2024 and 2025, about .095 each year.<sup>4</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">By our count of the play-by-play, about 486 runners were on base when he batted. He drove in 90 of them, nearly one in five, the best rate of the eight Cardinals with 300 or more plate appearances. Jordan Walker was next, at about 16%. Burleson's other nine RBI with men on were his own, on home runs, and 17 more came on solo home runs.<sup>7</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Left-handed pitchers</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Most of his damage came against right-handers. Burleson had a .939 OPS against them in 464 plate appearances and a .549 OPS against left-handers in 206. He drove in 76 runs against right-handers and 40 against left-handers.<sup>8</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Home and road</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">He also hit far better on the road (.890 OPS) than at Busch Stadium (.739).<sup>8</sup> Busch played low in 2026. Games there produced about 13% fewer runs than the Cardinals' road games, the fourth-lowest rate in the majors.<sup>9</sup> But the other Cardinals hitters' gap was much smaller, .652 at home and .697 on the road.<sup>10</sup> And Burleson had hit better at home in each of the three seasons before.<sup>8</sup> The park explains some of his split. We could not explain the rest.</p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">What we checked and set aside</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">RBI depend on the hitters who get on ahead of Burleson. This piece shows the chances he had and what he did with them. One season with men on base is a small sample, and we did not test why he hit better with men on.</p>
