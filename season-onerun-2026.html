Cardinals couldn’t keep it close

In 2026 there was no one with more losses in one-run games

Were the 2026 Cardinals unlucky? They lost 30 games by one run in 2026, more than any other team in the majors.1 The Cardinals had not lost that many since 1998.2

They lost so many mainly because they played so many.3 The Cardinals played 54 one-run games in 2026, tied with the Dodgers for the most in the majors. Eight teams had a worse one-run winning percentage.1

Their runs say the record was about right. The Cardinals scored 720 runs in 2026 and allowed 749. A standard formula turns runs into an expected record, and it gave them about 78 wins. They won 77.4

Better teams win more close games than worse teams do, but not many more. The way the Cardinals played their other games, they would be expected to go about 27-27 in 54 one-run games. They went 24-30, a normal miss.5

The Cardinals were outscored mostly in lopsided games.6 Flip one close loss to a win, and the record moves a full game. The run totals barely move.

Early luck, late luck

Through May 2026, the Cardinals went 31-26. Their runs predicted about 27 wins. From June on, they went 46-59, when their runs predicted about 51. The early luck and the late luck nearly canceled.7

Most of that swing came in one-run games: 11-5 through May, 13-25 after.7

The bullpen question

In 2026, 13 of the Cardinals’ 30 one-run losses included a blown save. Five of their 24 one-run wins did, too.8

Riley O’Brien saved 38 games in 2026. He blew 5 saves in games the Cardinals lost by one run, and 4 of those came after June.8 Over the full season, the Cardinals’ record matched their runs within one win.4 From June on, it fell about five wins short of them.7

Around the league

The Cardinals won about one game fewer than their runs predicted. Thirteen teams missed by more in 2026.9 The Brewers, who won the National League pennant,10 won 103 games in 2026, just what their runs predicted. They went 27-20 in one-run games.9

What carries over

A losing record tends to repeat the next season. A losing record in close games mostly does not.11

A team with a losing record was more than twice as likely as a winning team to have a losing record the next season. In one-run games, the gap nearly closes. Falling short of what the runs predicted barely carries over, either.11

Losing close games does follow losing in general. Since 1901, 62% of teams with a losing record in their other games also lost more one-run games than they won. Among teams with a winning record in their other games, 35% did.12

From 1901 through 2025, there were 576 team-seasons in which a club lost at least six more one-run games than it won and played again the next season. That next season, those clubs won 48.1% of their one-run games. They won 47.0% of all their games, still a little below average. Only 39% had a winning one-run record.13

Before 2026, the Cardinals had 22 such seasons. In 11 of them, the next season brought a winning one-run record. The 2023 Cardinals went 17-26 in one-run games. In 2024 they went 29-22. That year they won 83 games, though their runs predicted about 76.2

What we checked and set aside

The Cardinals went 9-10 in extra-inning games in 2026.14 A second common version of the expected-wins formula also gives them about 78 wins.4 We did not test whether the bullpen, the lineup or any decision caused the close losses.
