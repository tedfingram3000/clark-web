KEY MATCHUPS

The dominant story tonight is Chase Burns, and what the Cardinals have — and don't have — to work with against him. Burns enters 14-2 with a 2.47 ERA, and his 1.98 ERA at Great American Ball Park in 2026 (68.1 innings) makes home starts the most dangerous version of him. The Cardinals carry three combined career plate appearances against Burns. Tonight functions as a first encounter for every starter in the lineup.

Burns' 2026 platoon split sets the frame for how the lineup projects: he held right-handed batters to a .189 average and .500 OPS (189 BF) and left-handers to a .213 average and .676 OPS (337 BF). Seven of the nine Cardinals starters bat right-handed — the larger and more vulnerable group. Alec Burleson and JJ Wetherholt, the two left-handed bats, enter as the more historically favorable matchup based on Burns' 2025 left-handed numbers (.845 OPS), though his 2026 suppression of left-handers has been nearly as sharp.

Against Liberatore, the key individual advantage belongs to JJ Bleday: his .324 average and .500 slugging against left-handed pitching in 2025 (73 PA) is the strongest hitter-versus-handedness edge in tonight's game. Dane Myers (.286, .419 SLG vs. LHP, 117 PA) and Sal Stewart (.278 avg, .872 OPS vs. LHP in 2026, 126 PA) are the secondary targets in the Reds order. The Cardinals' starter will need to work through a right-handed lineup that includes the most aggressive base-stealing trio he'll face this series — McLain (18 SB, 90.0%), Myers (18 SB, 78.3%), and Hayes (12 SB, 80.0%).

The third lever is what Pedro Pagés does with that running game. His 31.5% caught-stealing rate on 54 attempts gives the Cardinals an active arm behind the plate, though McLain's 90.0% stolen-base success rate makes him the primary test. The game's defining throughline: a Cardinals lineup without answers on paper against a pitcher without a weakness on paper — and a Reds offense that will look to put pressure on Liberatore with speed early, against a Cardinals bullpen pacing at 469.0 innings and the most franchise-record exposure in the modern era.
