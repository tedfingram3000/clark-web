<div style="font-size:20px;font-weight:700;color:#0B1C33;line-height:1.3;margin:0 0 4px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The kids are here &#8211; don't book October yet, St. Louis</div>
<div style="font-size:13px;color:#707070;margin:0 0 14px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The youngest Cardinals lineup since 1917.<sup>1</sup> Young teams before them won about two more games a season.<sup>2</sup></div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">JJ Wetherholt led off in his major-league debut, the Cardinals' 2026 home opener at Busch Stadium.<sup>3</sup> In his second trip to the plate, he hit his first big-league home run.<sup>3</sup> By October he was a Gold Glove finalist at second base and a front-runner for NL Rookie of the Year.<sup>4</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Only two Cardinals had homered in a debut that was also the home opener: Eddie Morgan in 1936 and Wally Moon in 1954.<sup>5</sup> Wetherholt made it three.<sup>5</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Wetherholt was one of 13 players who made their major-league debuts for the Cardinals in 2026.<sup>6</sup> That tied eight other seasons for the most since 1924, when 14 did.<sup>6</sup> The latest was 2022.<sup>6</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">It was a young lineup.<sup>7</sup> Weighted by plate appearances, the Cardinals' 2026 hitters averaged 25.8 years old on June 30.<sup>7</sup> That was the youngest lineup the franchise has fielded since 1917, when the team averaged 25.7.<sup>1</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">History says to enjoy the kids and hold off on playoff plans. Young teams like this one won a little more over the next three years than teams with the same records.<sup>2</sup> They reached the postseason about as often.<sup>2</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Opening Day, then a walk-off</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The home opener was March 26, against Tampa Bay.<sup>3</sup> In the third inning, Wetherholt hit an estimated 425-foot home run off the Rays' Drew Rasmussen.<sup>3</sup> It was his first hit in the majors, and the sellout crowd called him back out for a curtain call.<sup>3</sup> Later, his sacrifice fly was part of an eight-run sixth inning.<sup>3</sup> The Cardinals came back from 7-1 down to win 9-7.<sup>3</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Two days later, he won a game with a walk-off hit.<sup>8</sup> His two-run single off Griffin Jax in the 10th inning beat the Rays 6-5.<sup>8</sup> Only two other Cardinals had a game-ending hit in one of their first two big-league games: Curt Ford in 1985 and Bill Virdon in 1955.<sup>9</sup> 700 Clark's Deep Scout for March 29 called his first two games an electrifying debut.<sup>8</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The everyday rookie</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Wetherholt came to the plate 619 times in 2026.<sup>10</sup> That was more than the other 12 debuts combined.<sup>10</sup> No Cardinal had more plate appearances in his debut season since Albert Pujols had 676 in 2001.<sup>11</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">He hit .242.<sup>10</sup> His 17 home runs were the most by a Cardinal in his debut season since Paul DeJong hit 25 in 2017.<sup>11</sup> He stole 14 bases in 15 tries.<sup>10</sup> MLB Pipeline named him the second baseman on its 2026 All-Rookie first team.<sup>4</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">His glove made the strongest case. A stat called outs above average counts the extra outs a fielder makes.<sup>12</sup> An average fielder is zero.<sup>12</sup> Statcast credited him with 25 outs above average in 2026, the most it has recorded for a rookie.<sup>12</sup> Only the Cubs' Pete Crow-Armstrong had more, with 26.<sup>12</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">He is also a finalist for the Gold Glove at second base.<sup>4</sup> The other two National League finalists are Milwaukee's Brice Turang and Pittsburgh's Brandon Lowe.<sup>4</sup> He and the Reds' Sal Stewart have led the betting for NL Rookie of the Year.<sup>4</sup> The last Cardinal to win it was Albert Pujols in 2001.<sup>4</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The loudest game</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The loudest single game came in August.<sup>13</sup> Joshua B&#225;ez saw one pitch in his first at-bat at Wrigley Field on Aug. 15 and hit it an estimated 449 feet.<sup>13</sup> He homered in his next two at-bats, too, the first player ever to homer in each of his first three.<sup>13</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">He came up from Triple-A Memphis, where his 34 home runs led Triple-A when he was called up.<sup>13</sup> The next two days were harder.<sup>13</sup> He went 0-for-14 with six strikeouts.<sup>13</sup> He finished 2026 with 7 home runs in 108 plate appearances, a small sample.<sup>10</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">More first days</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Six of the 13 debuts came on Aug. 1 or later.<sup>6</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Bryan Torres debuted at 28 on May 23 in Cincinnati.<sup>14</sup> He homered in the ninth inning of an 8-1 win, the first game of a doubleheader.<sup>14</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Blaze Jordan had two hits in his debut on June 12 in Minnesota.<sup>14</sup> The next day he tripled and homered.<sup>14</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Leo Bernal, a catcher, drove in 2 runs in his debut, a 13-8 win in Los Angeles on Sept. 1.<sup>14</sup> He homered the next day, too.<sup>14</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Quinn Mathews debuted on Aug. 1 in Toronto and allowed 2 runs in 5 innings.<sup>14</sup> He and Luis Gastelum threw the most innings of the 13.<sup>10</sup> Their lines are in the table at the end.<sup>10</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Cooper Hjerpe came out of the bullpen on Sept. 6 in Colorado.<sup>14</sup> He faced 12 batters, got 12 outs and struck out 6.<sup>14</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Young across the board</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Of the 30 teams, only the Nationals had a younger lineup in 2026, at 25.3.<sup>7</sup> The 29 teams other than St. Louis, taken together, averaged 28.4.<sup>7</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The pitching staff was young, too.<sup>7</sup> Weighted by innings, Cardinals pitchers averaged 27.9 years old.<sup>7</sup> That ranked fifth-youngest in the majors and was the youngest Cardinals staff since 2018.<sup>7</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Players under 30 hit 163 of the team's 167 home runs, or 97.6%.<sup>15</sup> Pitchers under 30 won 71 of its 77 games, or 92.2%.<sup>15</sup> No other team in 2026 reached 90% in both.<sup>15</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Young means different things in different eras.<sup>16</sup> Across the majors, lineups averaged as young as 27.4 years in 1912 and as old as 30.2 in 1945, the last war year.<sup>16</sup> So we also measured each Cardinals lineup against the rest of the majors in its own season.<sup>16</sup></p>

<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Against their own eras, only the 1907 and 1918 teams sat a little further below.<sup>16</sup> The 1938 team was about even.<sup>16</sup> In plain years, no Cardinals lineup since 1917 was younger than this one.<sup>1</sup></p>
<div style="font-size:10px;letter-spacing:2px;text-transform:uppercase;color:#0B1C33;font-weight:700;margin:16px 0 10px;padding-bottom:5px;border-bottom:2px solid #0B1C33;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">What happened to young teams</div>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">From 1901 through 2022, 95 major-league teams began a run of lineups at least 2 years younger than the rest of the majors.<sup>2</sup> Some stayed that young for years, so we count each run once, at its first season.<sup>2</sup> The 2026 Cardinals were 2.6 years younger.<sup>16</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">Over the next three seasons, those teams won about 2 more games per 162 than teams with similar records in the same era, a modest edge.<sup>2</sup> In the very next season, there was no clear edge.<sup>2</sup></p>

<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">The bars nearly match.<sup>2</sup> The young teams' postseason edge is too small to tell from chance.<sup>2</sup> The lower pairs narrow the field to records like the Cardinals' .475, from .430 to .520.<sup>2</sup> Thirty-one young teams fit, and they did about what the 913 other teams with those records did.<sup>2</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">A few examples show the range. The 2014 Chicago Cubs went 73-89.<sup>17</sup> In 2016 they won 103 games and the World Series.<sup>17</sup> The 1933 Detroit Tigers went 75-79, then won 101 and the pennant in 1934.<sup>17</sup> Others went the other way.<sup>17</sup> The 1975 Montreal Expos went 75-87, then lost 107 games in 1976.<sup>17</sup></p>
<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">These numbers describe what happened to past teams. They are not a forecast for 2027. Young lineups may also have been teams with good prospects on the way, and these numbers do not separate the two.</p>

<p style="font-size:13px;color:#2D3640;line-height:1.5;margin:0 0 10px;font-family:'Helvetica Neue',Helvetica,Arial,sans-serif;">We did not test whether the young roster explains the 77-85 record.<sup>2</sup> This piece makes no claim about it, and none about why the club built its roster this way.</p>

His exact age is the number of days from his birth date (birthYear, birthMonth and birthDay in the People table) to June 30 of the season, divided by 365.25.
